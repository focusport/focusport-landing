Sync Focus Sessions Between Your Windows PC and Android Phone

FocusPort Team • Published Oct 26, 2026 • 6 min read

Blocking your PC is only half a plan. Close the browser game on your laptop and the phone beside it is still one tap from the same feed. Most people do not lose a study session to the computer or the phone. They lose it in the hand-off between them.

This guide shows how to run a single focus session across a Windows PC and an Android phone with FocusPort, what is shared between them, and a routine that makes the hand-off hard to cheat.

Why one device is never enough

Single-device blocking trains you to swap devices. You hit the lock on the PC, reach for the phone, and spend the session there. A good setup removes that option: when the PC session starts, the phone is part of it too. That is the point of sharing sessions rather than running two separate timers.

What is shared

ItemShared between PC and phone
Focus sessionsYes. A session started on one device starts on the other while sync is in beta
SchedulesYes. A program made on your PC appears on Android too
Allowed and blocked listsYes
Blocking itselfRuns on each device, so a lost connection does not lift a session

Sync is optional and uses a cloud account, and it is free during the beta. Blocking itself is enforced on the device, which is why a session keeps running without internet.

Set it up in ten minutes

  1. Install FocusPort for Windows and FocusPort for Android.
  2. Sign in with the same account on both so they link.
  3. On the PC start screen, check that your phone shows as ready before you begin.
  4. Build one allowlist on each device: on the PC your work tools, on the phone only calls and the one or two apps you really need.
  5. Run a ten-minute test session and try to break it from each side.

A routine that works

  • Before: put the phone on the desk, face down, but still linked. Do not put it in another room, because then you will fetch it.
  • During: work in 50 to 90 minute blocks. Use Allowlist mode on the PC for deadline work and Blocklist for lighter tasks.
  • After: take a real break away from both screens. Your streak grows when the session finishes, so finishing matters more than length.
  • Weekly: schedule recurring programs for the hours you slip, for example 9 to 11 p.m. on weekdays.

Who gets the most out of it

Students revising for exams, remote workers whose phone is next to the keyboard, and anyone who has learned to dodge a PC-only blocker by switching screens. If you are a student, pair this with the phone study lock guide.

Limits to know about

The Windows version and cross-device sync are in beta, so you may meet rough edges. There is no iPhone version yet. And no tool can stop you from picking up a third device you did not link. Keep the setup simple, link what you actually use and test it once before a big week.
