Best App Blockers for Android in 2026: Tested Comparison

FocusPort Team • Published July 2, 2026 • 6 min read

Disclosure: I'm the developer of FocusPort, one of the apps in this comparison, so this isn't a fully independent review. That said, I actually installed and used AppBlock, StayFree, and Freedom myself before writing this — I wanted to know how FocusPort really stacks up, not just assume it's better. I've tried to call out where the other apps do things well, and where FocusPort still falls short.

Phone distractions can destroy productivity. For students trying to stay focused while studying or professionals working under deadlines, the urge to check notifications is a constant battle. This is where a robust Android app blocker becomes essential.

Not all app blockers are built the same way, though. Some only send gentle reminders, while others let you bypass your own restrictions with a single tap. Below is a hands-on comparison of the tools I tested — including the one I built.

Best App Blockers for Android in 2026 — At a Glance

  1. FocusPort — best for strict, hard-to-bypass focus sessions, completely free
  2. AppBlock — best for custom manual blocking rules
  3. Blockit — best for simple strict blocking with schedules and an allowlist
  4. StayFree — best for screen-time tracking and awareness
  5. Freedom — best for syncing blocks across phone and desktop
  6. Forest — best for gamified, gentle focus motivation (not a strict blocker)

1. FocusPort

I built FocusPort after running into the same problem with other blockers: either they were easy to switch off in a weak moment, or they were so rigid I stopped using them within days. The goal was a strict focus system that's genuinely hard to cheat, without being so inflexible that you abandon it.

FocusPort uses Android's Accessibility service and Device Admin protections to block distracting apps (Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, etc.) and prevent uninstallation during active sessions, while the Windows client blocks distracting PC processes and websites.

  • Strict Focus Lock: No quick disable button or "5 more minutes" option once a session starts.
  • Essential App Allowlist: Keep essential tools (calls, messaging, notes) accessible during a session.
  • Uninstall Protection: Built-in system-level uninstall protection prevents casual attempts to remove or bypass the app during active Android sessions.
  • Cross-Device Coverage: Multi-platform protection across Android and Windows.
  • Scheduled Sessions: Auto-start focus blocks during study or work hours.
  • Privacy & Offline: Core enforcement runs local, no usage data leaves your device.

Pros: Strict mode has no built-in escape hatch mid-session; Active-session uninstall protection on Android; Essential allowlist keeps critical tools usable; Cross-device support across Android and Windows; Fully free core blocking, no ads.

Cons: Preset-based blocking rather than arbitrary per-app complex scripting rules; Newer app, with desktop and mobile features continuously expanding.

2. AppBlock

AppBlock lets you create multiple usage profiles and block both apps and specific websites on a schedule. It's more customizable than FocusPort in terms of manual rule-building, and it's an established app with a large user base.

Pros: Most flexible manual rule-building of the group; Blocks specific websites, not just apps; Large, established user base.

Cons: Several useful features sit behind a paywall; Strict mode has to be configured carefully — on default settings, it was easier to disable mid-session than I expected.

3. Blockit

Blockit keeps the focus on app blocking with a genuinely strict mode. Recent updates added scheduling and an allowlist, so it now covers the basics that were missing before. For some users that simple setup is exactly the point.

Pros: Strict mode is genuinely hard to bypass; Simple to set up, with schedules and an allowlist now included.

Cons: Fewer extras than the bigger tools; Free version includes ads; Check the current feature list on Google Play, as the app has changed recently.

4. StayFree

StayFree is primarily a screen-time tracker with app limits layered on top. Its usage analytics are genuinely good for spotting patterns in your own behavior.

Pros: Best usage analytics and habit insights of the group; Good starting point if you don't yet know where your time actually goes; Free tier is genuinely usable.

Cons: Built around awareness, not hard enforcement — limits are easy to override in the moment; No scheduling or emergency-access system; Not a good fit if you need something that physically stops you from opening an app.

5. Freedom

Freedom's standout capability is multi-platform synchronization across both mobile and desktop platforms.

Pros: Multi-platform syncing across phone and desktop; Scheduling and strict mode both included.

Cons: On Android, blocking relies on a local VPN, which occasionally felt less stable in my testing and can be worked around by disabling the VPN in system settings; Subscription-only, no meaningful free tier; No emergency-access allowlist.

Not Strict Blockers, But Worth Knowing About

Not everyone needs a hard lock. If you just need a nudge rather than a wall, these two are worth a look.

Forest: A focus timer that grows a virtual tree while you stay off your phone — if you leave the app, the tree dies. It's motivating rather than enforcing, and works well for people who respond to gamification more than restriction.

one sec: Instead of blocking apps outright, one sec adds a short pause or breathing prompt before you open a distracting app, aiming to interrupt the automatic habit rather than prevent access entirely.

Who Should Choose FocusPort?

FocusPort tends to be the better fit if you:

  • Start a focus session and quit halfway through more often than you'd like.
  • Have uninstalled or disabled other blockers the moment temptation got strong.
  • Need a few essential apps (calls, banking, notes) to stay reachable during a session, not fully cut off.
  • Want sessions to start automatically on a schedule instead of remembering to turn blocking on.
  • Don't want to pay for basic strict blocking.

So Which One Should You Use?

There's no single best app blocker for everyone — it depends on what's actually not working in your current habits.

  • For habit tracking: Try StayFree.
  • For complex manual rule-building: AppBlock provides deep configuration.
  • For multi-platform desktop/mobile sync with VPN: Freedom.
  • For strict session enforcement without bypasses or paywalls: FocusPort.
