Best App Blockers for Android in 2026: The Complete Comparison Guide

Published on July 2, 2026 • 6 min read

Disclosure: I'm the developer of FocusPort, one of the apps in this comparison, so this isn't a fully independent review. That said, I actually installed and used AppBlock, StayFree, and Freedom myself before writing this — I wanted to know how FocusPort really stacks up, not just assume it's better. I've tried to call out where the other apps do things well, and where FocusPort still falls short.

Phone distractions can destroy productivity. For students trying to stay focused while studying or professionals working under deadlines, the urge to check notifications is a constant battle. This is where a robust Android app blocker becomes essential.

Not all app blockers are built the same way, though. Some only send gentle reminders, while others let you bypass your own restrictions with a single tap. Below is a hands-on comparison of the tools I tested — including the one I built.

Quick Comparison

1. FocusPort

I built FocusPort after running into the same problem with other blockers: either they were easy to switch off in a weak moment, or they were so rigid I stopped using them within days. The goal was a blocker that's genuinely hard to cheat, without being so inflexible that you abandon it.

FocusPort uses Android's Accessibility service to detect attempts to open blocked apps (Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, etc.) and redirects you back to your session instead of just nagging you.

  • Strict Mode Lock: No quick disable button or "5 more minutes" option once a session starts.
  • Emergency Whitelist: Keep essential tools (calls, banking, Notion) accessible during a session.
  • Scheduled Sessions: Auto-start blocks during study or work hours.
  • Privacy & Offline: Runs fully local, no usage data leaves your device.
  • Free: Core blocking and scheduling are free, no ads.

✅ Pros:

  • Strict mode has no built-in escape hatch mid-session.
  • Emergency whitelist keeps essential apps usable without breaking the block.
  • Scheduling works well for recurring study/work blocks.
  • Fully free, no ads, runs offline.

❌ Cons:

  • No per-app custom rule sets yet — sessions currently block your full preset list rather than mixing rules per app.
  • Newer app, so it doesn't have years of reviews or the install base that AppBlock or Freedom have.
  • Android only — no cross-device syncing with desktop yet.

2. AppBlock

AppBlock lets you create multiple usage profiles and block both apps and specific websites on a schedule. It's more customizable than FocusPort in terms of manual rule-building, and it's an established app with a large user base.

✅ Pros:

  • Most flexible manual rule-building of the group.
  • Blocks specific websites, not just apps.
  • Large, established user base.

❌ Cons:

  • Several useful features sit behind a paywall.
  • Strict mode has to be configured carefully — on default settings, it was easier to disable mid-session than I expected.

3. StayFree

StayFree is primarily a screen-time tracker with app limits layered on top. Its usage analytics are genuinely good for spotting patterns in your own behavior.

✅ Pros:

  • Best usage analytics and habit insights of the group.
  • Good starting point if you don't yet know where your time actually goes.
  • Free tier is genuinely usable.

❌ Cons:

  • Built around awareness, not hard enforcement — limits are easy to override in the moment.
  • No scheduling or emergency-access system.
  • Not a good fit if you need something that physically stops you from opening an app.

4. Freedom

Freedom's strongest feature is syncing blocks across your phone and computer, which none of the other apps here do.

✅ Pros:

  • Only app here with real cross-device syncing (phone + desktop).
  • Scheduling and strict mode both included.

❌ Cons:

  • On Android, blocking relies on a local VPN, which occasionally felt less stable in my testing and can be worked around by disabling the VPN in system settings.
  • Subscription-only, no meaningful free tier.
  • No emergency-access whitelist.

So Which One Should You Use?

There's no single best app blocker for everyone — it depends on what's actually not working in your current habits.

If you mainly want to understand your habits first:

Try StayFree — its tracking is the best of the group for spotting patterns before you commit to strict blocking.

If you want granular manual control and don't mind a paywall:

AppBlock gives you the most flexibility to build custom rules per app and schedule.

If you need blocking synced across phone and desktop:

Freedom is the only one here that does this well, if you're okay with the subscription and occasional VPN instability.

If your problem is specifically that you keep disabling your own blocks:

That's the exact problem FocusPort was built around — strict mode with no easy escape hatch, scheduled sessions, and emergency access for the apps you actually need.
